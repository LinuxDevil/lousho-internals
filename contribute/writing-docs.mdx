## Audience

A contributor who has **never seen this codebase** and may have never seen the domain. They can read TypeScript. They do not know what a checkpoint, a turn, or a `RunToolRegistry` is until the page tells them. Write for that reader — the expert skims anyway; the beginner is the one who quits.

## The page skeleton

Every subsystem page follows this order — concept before mechanics, mechanics before code:

```mdx
---
title: "<plain short name>"
description: "<what it does + why it exists, one sentence>"
---

## What this is
2-4 sentences in plain words. What it does for the SDK, plus one
familiar analogy. No jargon that isn't defined inline or linked.

## The mental model
A mermaid diagram + the 3-6 nouns the reader must hold.
("Three things exist: the gate, the store, the resume path.")

## How it works
A numbered walkthrough that literally follows the diagram's arrows.
Each step names the real function or file it maps to.

## Under the hood
The deep internals: orderings, invariants, failure modes. This is
where the verified technical density lives.

## In code
| Symbol | File | Role | — the entry-point table.

## Example
A real API snippet, narrated line by line.

## Design decisions
Bullets: what was chosen, what was rejected, what it costs.

## Where next
Links to the neighbouring pages a reader wants next.
```

Design-decision pages keep the tech-design template (Entry Point → Background → Goal → Scope → Solution → Other Solutions → Challenges → Implementation considerations) but put a `## The picture` mermaid diagram inside Solution and keep prose in plain sentences.

## Diagram rules

- Use ` ```mermaid ` fences — Mintlify renders them natively.
- `flowchart LR` or `flowchart TD` for structure, `sequenceDiagram` for lifecycles. Pick the one that matches the story.
- **≤ 10 nodes**, short labels (`Permission gate`, not `checkPermission() applies rule predicates`), quote labels containing `()`/`:`/`/`.
- The diagram must match the numbered walkthrough below it — a reader should be able to point at an arrow and find its paragraph.
- No ASCII-art walls. If a diagram needs >10 nodes, split the story into two diagrams.

## Voice rules

- Lead with the answer — the first sentence of every section says the thing, then explains it.
- One idea per paragraph; prefer tables over prose enumerations; keep lists under ~8 items.
- First use of an SDK term → link to its internals page or [the glossary](/glossary). First use of a general term → define it in plain words.
- Examples are narrated: say what each block does, not just what it is.
- Mark inference honestly: *(inferred)* when a rationale isn't recorded in code.
- No emojis, no marketing voice, no "Note that", no filler.
